## Failure observations

1. **Selection still loses useful entities.** Exact accepted-root entity IDs
   occur in at least one displayed non-C fact in 6/12 ranked, 9/12 coverage,
   and 5/12 local-contrast packets. This includes graph endpoints/onset rows,
   not necessarily causal or sufficiently diagnostic evidence. Candidate lists
   remain complete. Coverage can improve entity availability while lowering
   diagnostic accuracy; availability alone is insufficient.
2. **Unsupported relationships remain common.** Answers infer node hosting or
   pod ownership absent from the call-edge diagram, treat an unshown caller as
   proof of independence, or reverse caller/callee implications. The additional
   static warning did not reliably prevent this.
3. **Numbers and identities are misread.** Examples include assigning node
   properties to a three-digit service, calling a five-digit pod a node,
   interpreting 25.3 as earlier than 19.2, and moving a visible latency/metric
   value to a different entity. Even correct rankings can have unsupported
   explanations; a valid top-one prediction does not certify its rationale.
4. **Peak and onset heuristics can dominate.** Large capped deviation scores
   on low-baseline counters and ordinary log-volume fluctuations are repeatedly
   described as decisive faults. Earlier/largest is not automatically causal.
5. **The local-trace preference can remove an important independent metric.**
   For INC-34F03AB43950, the raft metric's entity is retained by ranked/coverage
   and omitted by local contrast; the first two answer correctly, the latter
   does not. This is a training example motivating a broader public policy,
   not permission to hardcode this case or its answer.
