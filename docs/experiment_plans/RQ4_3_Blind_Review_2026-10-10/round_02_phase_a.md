**Fixed rubric for later proposal assessment**

The following weights and scoring rules are frozen before proposal disclosure. Reviewer-derived principles above motivate the rubric; the detailed statistical procedures below are **my methodological requirements**, not quotations attributed to those reviewers.

| Dimension | Weight | What a ready plan must establish |
|---|---:|---|
| Scientific question and contribution | 15% | State a falsifiable question and distinguish the proposed contribution from existing selection, compression, representation or architecture ideas. Explain what is learned even if headline performance does not improve. Simplicity alone is not a defect. |
| Meaningful interventions and mechanism | 15% | Identify the manipulated variable, predicted result and disconfirming outcome. Include controls that distinguish the proposed mechanism from generic information loss, attention attraction, ordering, prompt bias or resource changes, where relevant. Observational attention or correlations alone cannot establish mechanism. |
| Fair controls and baseline fidelity | 20% | Match incident information, candidates, model settings and relevant budgets; document unavoidable differences. Include strong practical baselines and simple alternatives. Measure effectiveness and efficiency for the same configuration. Give adaptations to prior methods their own explicit identity. |
| Statistical validity and selection discipline | 15% | Prespecify primary contrasts, estimands and denominators; account for paired cases and shared incident/source groups. Provide uncertainty, relevant effect-size or noninferiority margins, and a sample-size justification. Separate development from confirmation, disclose historical exposure, and handle multiple comparisons and stochastic repeats appropriately. Repeats are not independent incident samples. |
| End-to-end deployment efficiency | 15% | Specify hardware/software and include preprocessing, rendering/encoding where applicable, inference and failures/retries. Report measured latency distributions, memory and throughput for relevant sequence lengths and concurrency; distinguish prefill from generation. Token counts, asymptotic complexity and isolated kernels are supporting measurements. |
| Robustness and transfer | 10% | Define the population to which claims apply. Test relevant variation in density, temporal structure, chart layout, entity count and model family, as appropriate. Separate perceptual failures from reasoning failures and aggregate gains from serious subgroup regressions. |
| Reproducibility and feasibility | 10% | Specify executable arms, immutable inputs/configurations, provenance, scorer rules, failure accounting, budget, stopping criteria and a feasible qualification sequence. Preserve null and adverse results. |

Each dimension receives a 1–10 score; the readiness score is their weighted mean, reported to one decimal with explicit unresolved issues:

- **1–2:** Invalid or insufficiently specified.
- **3–4:** Major redesign required.
- **5–6:** Plausible study with substantial unresolved validity or execution issues.
- **7–8:** Ready for a bounded, informative study; remaining limitations are explicit and manageable.
- **9:** Exceptionally well specified, with strong controls and feasible execution.
- **10:** Reserved for an unusually complete plan with no material unresolved design issue; it still cannot guarantee positive findings.

Overrides are applied transparently rather than hidden in averaging: unresolved label leakage, an invalid comparison or a failure denominator that can reverse the central claim caps readiness at **4**. A central efficiency claim without a planned measurement of total deployed cost caps readiness at **5**. An explicitly causal mechanism claim without a discriminating intervention also caps readiness at **5**; narrowing that claim can resolve this issue.

Publication recommendation will be reported separately. A rigorous, executable plan can earn high readiness while its eventual results remain insufficiently novel or consequential for publication. Historical wins, desired thresholds and other reviewers’ scores will not substitute for evidence.
