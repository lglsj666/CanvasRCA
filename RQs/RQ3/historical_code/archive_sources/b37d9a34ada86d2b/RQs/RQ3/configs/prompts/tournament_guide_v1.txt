Reading the supplied dashboard:

M = metrics; R = traces; L = logs; G = directed topology. Each bordered outer card groups displayed telemetry. Position and color do not identify the correct candidate.

Some metric labels explicitly print SERVICE, NODE or POD before the owner ID. These words state the owner's entity type; they are not part of the metric name. For example, M16 NODE 1234 · system.cpu.user means series M16, owned by node 1234, measuring system.cpu.user.

M labels follow M<series-number> <owner-ID> · <metric-name>. M numbers identify series, not entities or root-cause ranks. The entire suffix after · is a metric name. Each horizontal lane has its own source-unit vertical scale: equal line heights in different lanes do not mean equal values. Lines show 64 relative bins, b0 through b63, across the observation window. The pale band is the telemetry-estimated anomalous interval. The printed base is the comparison baseline; peak is the largest displayed departure from it; z is signed standardized peak deviation, not a probability and not a mean shift. Use numerical magnitudes and shapes together. A series can have a large z but a tiny absolute change. The header gives relative interval, complete observation duration, and source-row count. Times are relative, not calendar times. k/M/G mean thousand/million/billion; ms/s/m mean milliseconds/seconds/minutes. When MET-Z statistics are explicitly supplied, regular/current mean and standard deviation describe those periods; do not substitute the printed peak for current mean.

R labels are <owner-ID> · <operation-name>. A numeric endpoint inside the operation name is not necessarily the owning entity. Each row prints count baseline -> current and exclusive p95 latency baseline -> current in milliseconds. p95 means the 95th percentile. Δlog2 means a base-two fold change: +1 doubles, -1 halves; a change close to zero is small. Green/red points are baseline/current. Their positions use a log(1+latency) scale; read printed values for exact ratios. The local-latency estimate is evidence to weigh, not guaranteed proof of a local fault.

L rows give an LT template ID, owner entity, relative bin, event count, severity and normalized message. Placeholders identify variable slots. Log variable summaries give n occurrences, values×frequency, or first/last/min/max/distinct. They are observed data, not diagnosis scores. LOG-R error/event rates compare baseline -> current for that owner; volume_drop and new_errors describe those measured changes. A request-started/completed message is not itself an error. A point on the row's timeline locates that template/bin, not the entire case-wide rate comparison.

G arrows and exact edge-ledger rows mean caller -> callee. Use the ledger to check direction. They are call edges, not deployment/hosting edges. The onset list prints display rank, entity, estimated relative onset in minutes, source-specific z, and src M or R. Rank is not causal priority; src names the detecting modality. Coincident onset does not establish ordering. An entry early in the recording is not necessarily the incident origin. A displayed subset of edges cannot prove that an unshown edge does not exist.


The NAME GROUPS strip in G pairs a 3-digit service alias with its 5-digit pod-name members. This public naming projection links different telemetry naming levels; it is not a call edge or node-hosting relationship. A service-wide trace change need not identify which member pod is faulty. Use each metric/log row's own printed owner ID to distinguish members. Other possible candidates remain in the text candidate list; the strip is not a list of recommended answers.

Cards and alternative visual encodings:
Each bordered outer card is one evidence bundle. A modality card groups one source type; an incident card groups this case's selected evidence; an evidence card groups related observations across sources. In a temporal arrangement, large card numbers specify chronological order of the labelled intervals, not anomaly priority. EC numbers are source addresses only; M-series numbers and LT-template numbers identify observations within cards. Never infer an entity's type from the number of digits in a card, series or template label.

Metric heatmaps use the printed colour scale, with blue above the scale midpoint and green below it; read numbers and the scale together. Time-bin bars retain temporal order. Overlaid lines share an axis only for compatible metric meanings and units; use their legends. Each separately framed plot may have its own scale. Trace paired bars or connected points compare the printed baseline/current values; the log(1+latency) axis is not a raw latency ratio. Template-time matrices retain the accompanying log text and counts. In graph adjacency matrices, rows are callers and columns are callees. Layered graphs can retain cycles and isolated nodes. Tables express the same printed values and relationships. Layout and resolution may vary: follow the M/R/L/G labels rather than assuming fixed screen coordinates.
