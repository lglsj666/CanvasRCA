Reading the supplied dashboard:

M = metrics; R = traces; L = logs; G = directed topology. Each bordered outer card groups displayed telemetry. Position and color do not identify the correct candidate.

M labels give M<series-number>, an owner type SERVICE/NODE/POD and owner ID, followed by the full metric name on wrapped lines. M numbers identify series, not entities or root-cause ranks. Each horizontal lane has its own source-unit vertical scale: equal line heights in different lanes do not mean equal values. Lines show 64 relative bins, b0 through b63, across the complete observation window. An optional pale band is a heuristic timing summary, not a known fault boundary. The printed base is the comparison baseline; peak is the largest displayed departure from it; z is signed standardized peak deviation, not a probability and not a mean shift. Use numerical magnitudes and shapes together. A series can have a large z but a tiny absolute change. The header gives observation duration and source-row count. Times are relative, not calendar times. k/M/G mean thousand/million/billion; ms/s/m mean milliseconds/seconds/minutes. When MET-Z statistics are explicitly supplied, regular/current mean and standard deviation describe those comparison periods; do not substitute the printed peak for current mean.

R labels are <owner-ID> · <operation-name>. A numeric endpoint inside the operation name is not necessarily the owning entity. Each row prints count baseline -> current and exclusive p95 latency baseline -> current in milliseconds. p95 means the 95th percentile. Δlog2 means a base-two fold change: +1 doubles, -1 halves; a change close to zero is small. Green/red points are baseline/current. Their positions use a log(1+latency) scale; read printed values for exact ratios. The local-latency estimate is evidence to weigh, not guaranteed proof of a local fault.

L rows give an LT template ID, owner entity, relative bin, event count, severity and normalized message. Placeholders identify variable slots. Log variable summaries give n occurrences, values×frequency, or first/last/min/max/distinct. They are observed data, not diagnosis scores. LOG-R error/event rates compare baseline -> current for that owner; volume_drop and new_errors describe those measured changes. A request-started/completed message is not itself an error. A point on the row's timeline locates that template/bin, not the entire case-wide rate comparison.

G arrows and exact edge-ledger rows mean caller -> callee. Use the ledger to check direction. They are call edges, not deployment/hosting edges. An optional onset list gives display rank, entity, estimated relative onset in minutes, source-specific z, and src M or R. Rank is not causal priority; src names the detecting modality. Read actual series for temporal evidence: the onset list is not a ground-truth fault order. A displayed subset of edges cannot prove that an unshown edge does not exist.
